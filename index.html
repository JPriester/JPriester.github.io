Research Overview

High-Level Research Directions

Rather than treating learning and control as separate modules, my research embeds formal dynamical systems structure directly into deep reinforcement learning. Explore the three core pillars below through visual animations and accessible research articles.

Direction I • Hysteresis-Based RL & Hybrid Supervision HyRL • MultiHyRL • L-MultiHyRL • Timer Supervision

Hysteresis-Based Reinforcement Learning for Chatter-Free, Robust Autonomy

Overview: Standard neural network policies parameterize continuous, memoryless mappings from observations to actions. When an autonomous vehicle navigates around obstacles or switches among competing tasks, topological obstructions create critical boundaries where opposing trajectories diverge. Along these boundaries, small sensor noise or adversarial perturbations shift the perceived obstacle into the vehicle's active passage direction—tricking a memoryless agent into rapidly oscillating between conflicting routes (chattering) and crashing. Across our Hysteresis-Based Reinforcement Learning framework, we embed specialized RL policies inside hybrid dynamical systems using overlapping spatial hysteresis partitions and predictive dwell-time timers to guarantee decisive, chatter-free trajectory commitment.

✨ Key Takeaway

Continuous feedback laws—including recurrent policies (RNNs) and frame stacking—cannot eliminate critical switching boundaries around obstacles without discrete memory. Across our HyRL, MultiHyRL, and L-MultiHyRL framework, we partition the state space around obstacles into overlapping domains so that the agent locks in its initialized route and ignores perturbation-induced shortcuts—achieving 100% collision-free 2D and 3D navigation. When value boundaries are fragmented or unknown in advance, our Timer-Based Hybrid Supervisor predicts forward value trajectories to adaptively lock in the active policy until the agent safely clears the vulnerable region.

Direction II • Theoretical Foundations of RL Closed-Loop Stability • Continuous Control

Intrinsic Practical Stability in Reinforcement Learning

Overview: Many continuous control tasks in robotics—such as legged locomotion gaits, autonomous drone racing, and dexterous manipulation—are fundamentally set-stabilization problems that require steering a system toward and keeping it within a desired target behavior. Existing safe RL methods typically modify the learning algorithm with Lyapunov constraints or external safety filters, which can restrict exploration and complicate training. In our RLC 2026 paper, we prove that modifying the core RL objective is often unnecessary to guarantee the stability of the deployed policy: standard discounted RL intrinsically guarantees practical asymptotic stability.

✨ Key Takeaway

Why do standard RL agents often settle into a small steady-state error near the target? Rather than being an algorithmic flaw, this offset is a natural consequence of discounting future rewards, which makes the agent slightly myopic and content with being "close enough." By modeling both this discounted horizon and neural network approximation errors as bounded disturbances, we prove that near-optimal neural policies naturally steer the system into a tight, bounded neighborhood around the target—which shrinks to zero as network approximation improves and the agent becomes more farsighted.

Direction III • Physical Robotics & Sim-to-Real Validation ModalAI Sentinel UAV (L-MultiHyRL) • 6-DoF Manipulator (CCTA '26)

From Theory & Simulation to Physical Robots: Real-World Robustness Validation

Overview: While much of our foundational research is rooted in formal control theory and simulation, a central goal of this direction is demonstrating that the mathematical robustness we design scales to the real world and works reliably on physical hardware. Real-world robots face unmodeled aerodynamics, actuator delays, sensor drift, and physical contact compliance. Two experimental works validate that our hysteresis-switching principles transfer directly to hardware and eliminate real-world chattering:

🚁 Flagship RL Hardware Validation • L-MultiHyRL Quadrotor Flight

In our flagship sim-to-real study, we deploy L-MultiHyRL zero-shot onto a physical ModalAI Sentinel quadrotor. Under adversarial observation perturbations and $\text{SO}(3)$ antipodal rotation boundaries where standard continuous RL policies chatter, stall, and crash, our hysteresis-based RL architecture transfers seamlessly across the sim-to-real gap—achieving collision-free 3D multi-obstacle flight and smooth attitude convergence.

🤖 Classical Hybrid Counterpart • 6-DoF Contact & Force Control (CCTA '26)

While not RL-based, our collaborative robotics study on a physical 6-DoF manipulator applies the same hybrid hysteresis-switching principle to prevent chattering between position and force controllers. Embedding hysteresis thresholds eliminates contact bouncing and maintains stable grasping under changing object compliance, shifting centers of gravity, and long-term sensor drift.

  1. Preprint
    Latent Multi-Policy Hysteresis-Based Reinforcement Learning for Robust 3D Obstacle Avoidance
    Jan de Priester, Joseph Lullo, Kyle Volle, and 2 more authors
    2026
    Manuscript in preparation / under review (Hardware validated on ModalAI Sentinel quadrotor)
    Article Abs

    Deep reinforcement learning (RL) enables agile autonomous navigation from onboard sensory data, but standard continuous and memoryless RL-based policies lack robustness in topologically obstructed spaces. Specifically, when bypassing obstacles in SE(3) or tracking attitudes in SO(3), topological obstructions create decision boundaries where perturbations can induce rapid switching (chattering) between passage routes or rotation directions, trapping the vehicle at unaligned attitudes or causing collisions. We propose Latent Multi-Policy Hysteresis-Based RL (L-MultiHyRL) for robust, perception-driven 3D obstacle avoidance and attitude tracking, achieving 100% collision-free navigation under adversarial observation perturbations in 3D simulations and physical ModalAI Sentinel quadrotor flights.

  2. RLC
    Intrinsic Closed-Loop Practical Asymptotic Stability in Discrete-Time Reinforcement Learning
    Jan de Priester and Ricardo G. Sanfelice
    In Reinforcement Learning Conference (RLC), 2026
    Accepted, to appear
    Article Abs PDF

    Deep reinforcement learning (RL) has achieved impressive empirical success in complex control tasks, many of which are fundamentally set-stabilization problems. While existing methods often modify RL algorithms to enforce strict asymptotic stability, we offer a complementary perspective: modifying the core RL objective is often unnecessary to guarantee the stability of the deployed policy. We prove that the unmodified discounted RL objective intrinsically guarantees closed-loop uniform practical asymptotic stability (UPAS) in deterministic continuous control settings. By adapting the discrete-time input-to-state stability (ISS) framework, we model the suboptimality induced by the discount factor and neural network approximation error as bounded disturbances, proving that near-optimal parameterized feedback laws steer the closed-loop system into a bounded neighborhood of the target set that vanishes as approximation error approaches zero and the discount factor approaches one.

  3. RLC
    A Timer-Based Hybrid Supervisor for Robust, Chatter-Free Policy Switching
    Jan de Priester and Ricardo G. Sanfelice
    In Reinforcement Learning Conference (RLC), 2025
    Article Abs PDF

    We address the challenge of switching among multiple learned policies in reinforcement learning control systems, where conventional value function-based selection can induce rapid chattering under small measurement noise. We propose a predictive timer-based hybrid supervisor that integrates a resettable timer enforcing an adaptive dwell time on the active policy by predicting forward trajectories, rendering the target set robustly globally asymptotically stable.

  4. RLC
    MultiHyRL: Robust Hybrid RL for Obstacle Avoidance against Adversarial Attacks on the Observation Space
    Jan de Priester, Zachary Bell, Prashant Ganesh, and 1 more author
    In Reinforcement Learning Conference (RLC), 2024
    Abs PDF

    Standard continuous reinforcement learning policies lack formal robustness guarantees against adversarial perturbations in the observation space during obstacle avoidance, where topological obstructions create vulnerable decision boundaries. MultiHyRL identifies critical points of a baseline policy and constructs overlapping relaxed Voronoi partitions with hysteresis switching, guaranteeing robust navigation across multi-obstacle environments.
