Hysteresis-Based Reinforcement Learning for 2D & 3D Obstacle Avoidance

Overcoming Topological Chattering in Autonomous Navigation Across HyRL, MultiHyRL, and L-MultiHyRL

📄 Research Overview: HyRL (ACC 2022) • MultiHyRL (RLC 2024) • L-MultiHyRL (2026)

This article provides a unified overview of our line of research on Hysteresis-Based Reinforcement Learning for robust obstacle avoidance—spanning our published foundational algorithms in HyRL (ACC 2022) (de Priester et al., 2022) and MultiHyRL (RLC 2024) (de Priester et al., 2024), and highlighting simulation and physical quadrotor flight results from our latest extension to 3D aerial navigation, L-MultiHyRL (de Priester et al., 2026) (manuscript under review; full algorithmic details to appear upon publication): [ACC 2022 PDF →] [RLC 2024 PDF →]

Deep reinforcement learning (RL) enables agile autonomous navigation directly from onboard sensors. However, standard RL policies parameterize continuous, memoryless mappings from observations to actions—a fundamental limitation that makes them vulnerable to topological obstructions in safety-critical obstacle avoidance.

Whenever an autonomous vehicle approaches an obstacle on its way to a target, it must break symmetry and commit to a bypass route (for example, passing above/below or clockwise/counterclockwise in 2D, or maneuvering in 3D space). Because the policy is continuous, this directional choice creates a vulnerable set of critical points $\mathcal{M}^\ast$ directly ahead of the obstacle where opposing trajectories diverge. Along this boundary, small sensor noise or adversarial perturbations shift the perceived obstacle into the vehicle’s active passage direction—making the opposing bypass route appear shorter and tricking a memoryless agent into rapidly reversing its decision (chattering) until it collides head-on with the obstacle.

Across HyRL (de Priester et al., 2022), MultiHyRL (de Priester et al., 2024), and L-MultiHyRL (de Priester et al., 2026), our core insight is simple:

Continuous neural networks cannot robustly break symmetry around obstacles without discrete memory. By partitioning the state space into overlapping regions and embedding specialized RL policies inside a closed-loop hybrid system with hysteresis switching, Hysteresis-Based Reinforcement Learning eliminates decision chattering and guarantees decisive, collision-free obstacle avoidance under worst-case observation noise.

1. How Hysteresis-Based RL Works: Overlapping Partitions & Hysteresis Logic

How do we systematically eliminate topological chattering without hand-crafting avoidance controllers? Beginning with our ACC 2022 (de Priester et al., 2022) and RLC 2024 (de Priester et al., 2024) papers, we established a general hysteresis-based reinforcement learning framework built on three steps:

  1. Empirical Critical Point Identification: Starting from a single baseline RL policy, we probe the environment with small opposing state perturbations $\pm \nu$. States from which perturbed rollouts split around opposite sides of an obstacle reveal the baseline policy’s vulnerable critical region $\mathcal{M}^\ast$ (panel a below).
  2. Overlapping State-Space Partitioning: We bisect the critical region $\mathcal{M}^\ast$ into two domains $\mathcal{M}\sb{1}$ (e.g., an Upper Partition) and $\mathcal{M}\sb{2}$ (e.g., a Lower Partition) and expand each across the centerline boundary by a margin $\rho$ to create an overlapping hysteresis region ($\mathcal{M}\sb{1}^{\text{exp}}$ intersecting $\mathcal{M}\sb{2}^{\text{exp}}$) whose width exceeds the worst-case sensor or adversarial perturbation (panels b and c below).
  3. Dedicated Policies & Hysteresis Switching Logic: Two specialized RL policies are trained on the overlapping domains—one committed to passing each side of the obstacle. A discrete logic state locks in the initialized policy inside the overlap and ignores perturbation-induced shortcuts, only permitting a switch if the vehicle moves completely outside the active policy’s expanded partition.
Constructing Overlapping Hysteresis Regions: (a) Small opposing perturbations $z_0 \pm \nu$ cause baseline trajectories $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$ to diverge around the obstacle, identifying critical points. (b) The empirical critical points $\mathcal{M}^\ast$ (red dots) are bisected into $\mathcal{M}\sb{1}$ and $\mathcal{M}\sb{2}$ and expanded by $\rho_1$ to create an overlap buffer. (c) Inside the overlap between $\mathcal{M}\sb{1}^{\text{exp}}$ and $\mathcal{M}\sb{2}^{\text{exp}}$, both specialized policies safely route the vehicle around opposite sides of the obstacle without chattering.
Animation 2: How Overlapping Partitions & Hysteresis Lock In the Active Route ● Hysteresis Switching Logic
Eliminating Route Chattering via Overlapping Spatial Partitions: Phase 1 (Overlapping Spatial Partitions Create Hysteresis): The Upper Partition (blue) and Lower Partition (green) extend across the centerline to form a central Overlapping Region where both bypass routes are valid. Phase 2 (Initialized Upper Route Commits Despite Upward Shift): When initialized to the Upper Route policy (blue), the obstacle shifts upward—making the lower path geometrically shorter (red dashed curve). Because the vehicle remains inside the Upper Partition, it ignores the shorter route and commits to the upper trajectory with 0 route reversals. Phase 3 (Initialized Lower Route Commits Despite Downward Shift): Symmetrically, when initialized to the Lower Route policy (green) and confronted with a downward obstacle shift, the vehicle stays inside the Lower Partition and commits smoothly beneath the obstacle with 0 route reversals.

  • MultiHyRL (RLC 2024) (de Priester et al., 2024) generalized the framework to cluttered multi-obstacle 2D arenas and local bird’s-eye-view visual observations (below right), combining a CNN-MLP perception architecture with directional hysteresis and obstacle-focus switching across relaxed Voronoi cells.

Right (MultiHyRL — RLC 2024, Figure 2 (de Priester et al., 2024)): Closed-loop control architecture combining local bird's-eye view CNN features with relative target error MLP features.

2. Extending to 3D Flight & Attitude Tracking: L-MultiHyRL Results (2026)

In our latest work on Latent Multi-Policy Hysteresis-Based Reinforcement Learning (L-MultiHyRL) (de Priester et al., 2026), we extend hysteresis-based reinforcement learning from 2D planar navigation to full 3D aerial flight in $\text{SE}(3)$ and rotational attitude tracking in $\text{SO}(3)$ using local onboard visual observations.

In 3D flight, a vehicle faces two simultaneous topological obstructions:

  1. 3D Spatial Obstacle Avoidance: Breaking symmetry around 3D obstacles in cluttered environments under position observation noise $\varepsilon_p$.
  2. 3D Attitude Tracking on $\text{SO}(3)$: Because antipodal unit quaternions $\pm q_e$ represent the same physical 3D orientation via opposite rotation directions, continuous policies encounter a critical switching boundary at a $180^\circ$ rotation error ($\eta_e = 0$), where attitude noise $\varepsilon_q$ induces violent angular velocity chattering.

(Because the L-MultiHyRL manuscript is currently under review, we omit the internal algorithmic and architectural details here and highlight the 3D simulation and physical hardware validation results below.)

3D Simulation Benchmark: Single- & Multi-Obstacle Navigation

Across 1,000 randomized 3D initial conditions under worst-case adversarial observation shifts ($\varepsilon_p = 0.20\text{ m}$) and attitude disturbances ($\varepsilon_q = 0.4$), Standard PPO suffers from severe chattering—colliding with obstacles in 3D space and stalling at unaligned orientations near $\eta_e = 0$. In contrast, L-MultiHyRL achieves 100.0% collision-free convergence across every tested noise level:

3D Attitude Tracking Under Adversarial Noise ($\varepsilon_q = 0.4$): Left (Standard PPO): Initial orientations near the antipodal boundary ($\eta_e(0)$ near $0$) are trapped at $\eta_e = 0$ as angular velocity commands $u_y$ and $u_z$ chatter at maximum frequency. Right (L-MultiHyRL): Hybrid hysteresis eliminates control chattering and smoothly stabilizes all initial orientations.
3D Multi-Obstacle Navigation Under Adversarial Observation Perturbations ($\varepsilon_p = 0.20\text{ m}$): Top-down ($p_x$-$p_y$, left) and side ($p_x$-$p_z$, right) projections. Standard PPO (red dashed) chatters and collides with the spherical obstacles (black circles), while L-MultiHyRL smoothly routes all trajectories to the target (gold star) via committed bypass modes (blue and green).
3D Cumulative Success Rate Over Time (1,000 Runs per Condition): As the adversarial perturbation magnitude increases from $\varepsilon_p = 0.00$ to $\varepsilon_p = 0.20$, Standard PPO (solid curves) degrades to 60.4% success in the single-obstacle arena and 68.6% in the multi-obstacle arena. L-MultiHyRL (dashed curves) maintains 100.0% collision-free convergence across all noise levels.

3. Physical Flight Validation on a ModalAI Sentinel Quadrotor

To verify that these robustness gains transfer directly to real-world aerial hardware under aerodynamic disturbances and closed-loop tracking delays, we deployed L-MultiHyRL zero-shot onto a physical ModalAI Sentinel quadrotor inside an indoor motion-capture flight arena.

Standard PPO (red) suffers from mid-flight indecision and collides with the obstacles (black circles), whereas L-MultiHyRL (blue and green) executes decisive, collision-free 3D avoidance maneuvers in every flight trial.
Physical Quadrotor Attitude Tracking Under Perturbations ($\varepsilon_q = 0.4$): Left (Standard PPO): Initial orientations near the antipodal boundary trigger severe hardware angular rate chattering and stall near $\eta_e = 0$. Right (L-MultiHyRL): Hybrid hysteresis eliminates chattering on the physical quadrotor and drives all trajectories smoothly to the desired orientation.

🛠️ Practical Impact & Applications

From 2D state-based HyRL (ACC 2022) (de Priester et al., 2022) and 2D vision-based MultiHyRL (RLC 2024) (de Priester et al., 2024) to 3D L-MultiHyRL (2026) (de Priester et al., 2026), our hysteresis-based reinforcement learning framework provides a principled upgrade for safety-critical autonomous systems—including agile UAV flight in cluttered environments, vision-based obstacle avoidance under sensor spoofing or occlusion, and large-angle 3D attitude maneuvers.
