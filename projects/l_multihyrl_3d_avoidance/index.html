Latent Multi-Policy Hybrid RL for 3D Obstacle Avoidance

Overcoming Spatial and Attitude Chattering in SE(3) via Latent Feature Partitioning and Dual Hysteresis

📄 Research Article & Hardware Validation (2026)

This article provides an accessible overview of our work, "Latent Multi-Policy Hysteresis-Based Reinforcement Learning for Robust 3D Obstacle Avoidance" by Jan de Priester, Joseph Lullo, Kyle Volle, Prashant Ganesh, and Ricardo G. Sanfelice (de Priester et al., 2026), building upon our hybrid RL foundations in HyRL (ACC 2022) (de Priester et al., 2022) and MultiHyRL (RLC 2024) (de Priester et al., 2024).

Deep reinforcement learning (RL) enables agile autonomous navigation directly from onboard sensors. However, standard RL policies are continuous, memoryless mappings from observations to actions—a structural limitation that makes them vulnerable to topological obstructions during obstacle avoidance.

Whenever an autonomous vehicle approaches an obstacle in 3D space, it must break symmetry and commit to a bypass route (e.g., passing left/right or over/under). Along the set of critical points $\mathcal{M}^*$ dividing these equally viable routes, small sensor noise or adversarial perturbations can repeatedly flip the policy’s decision. Instead of committing to a safe path around the obstacle, the vehicle chatters rapidly between opposing commands and collides head-on.

Left (Chattering Along Critical Points $\mathcal{M}^*$): When a vehicle approaches an obstacle along the critical dividing line (red dashed), small observation noise causes a memoryless policy to oscillate between passing left (blue) and passing right (green), crashing into the obstacle. Right (Hysteresis Across Overlapping Domains): Partitioning the space around the obstacle into overlapping regions $\mathcal{M}_1^{\text{exp}}$ and $\mathcal{M}_2^{\text{exp}}$ allows a hybrid supervisor to lock in a dedicated policy inside the overlap $\mathcal{M}_1^{\text{exp}} \cap \mathcal{M}_2^{\text{exp}}$.

In our earlier work, we resolved this vulnerability in 2D planar settings:

  • HyRL (ACC 2022) (de Priester et al., 2022) introduced hysteresis-based reinforcement learning, showing that training two policies on overlapping contractible subsets around an obstacle—and switching between them via a discrete logic variable—eliminates chattering when global obstacle coordinates are known.
  • MultiHyRL (RLC 2024) (de Priester et al., 2024) extended this framework to multi-obstacle 2D environments with local bird’s-eye-view perception, identifying critical points empirically from a single baseline policy and training a separate classifier to estimate overlapping regions from raw 2D images.

Scaling hybrid RL from 2D planar navigation to full 6-DoF aerial flight in $\text{SE}(3)$ introduces two major challenges: (1) rotational tracking on $\text{SO}(3)$ suffers from its own topological obstruction— causing severe attitude chattering—and (2) training separate image classifiers for 3D spatial partitions is computationally redundant.

In this work, we introduce Latent Multi-Policy Hysteresis-Based RL (L-MultiHyRL) (de Priester et al., 2026):

L-MultiHyRL unifies 3D spatial and rotational anti-chattering inside a single closed-loop hybrid system. By predicting overlapping spatial partitions directly from the baseline policy’s learned latent CNN features and pairing spatial policy switching with quaternion pole hysteresis, L-MultiHyRL achieves chatter-free 3D obstacle avoidance in both simulation and physical quadrotor flight tests.

1. Dual Topological Obstructions in 3D Flight ($\text{SE}(3)$ and $\text{SO}(3)$)

We consider an autonomous aerial vehicle operating in $Z := \text{SE}(3) = \mathbb{R}^3 \times \text{SO}(3)$ with position $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and orientation represented by a unit quaternion $q = (\eta, \epsilon) \in \mathbb{S}^3$. To navigate using local onboard sensing without a global map, the policy receives global tracking errors $(e_p, q_e)$ combined with two orthogonal body-aligned 2D binary obstacle projections $\mathcal{V}(z) = (\mathcal{V}{xy}(z), \mathcal{V}{xz}(z))$ capturing the local horizontal (top-down) and vertical (side) fields of view:

Multimodal Observation Space in $\text{SE}(3)$: (a) Isometric view of the vehicle with position error $e_p$, quaternion attitude error $q_e$, and orthogonal body-aligned sensing planes $\mathcal{V}_{xy}$ (cyan) and $\mathcal{V}_{xz}$ (magenta). (b) The resulting $64 \times 64$ binary visual projections $\mathcal{V} = (\mathcal{V}_{xy}, \mathcal{V}_{xz})$. (c) Geometry of an adversarial observation shift $\varepsilon_p \hat{\nu}_{\text{int}}$ perturbing the perceived obstacle position across the vehicle-to-target line of sight.

Why do continuous RL policies fail in $\text{SE}(3)$ under bounded sensor or adversarial perturbations $(\varepsilon_p, \varepsilon_q)$? Because two distinct topological obstructions arise simultaneously:

  1. Spatial Chattering Around 3D Obstacles ($\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \mathcal{O}$): Any continuous policy must contain a critical switching surface $\mathcal{M}^*$ ahead of each obstacle separating opposing 3D bypass trajectories. Note that recurrent policies (RNNs/LSTMs) or frame stacking do not eliminate this obstruction: because a fixed-window history mapping remains continuous on a connected product space, a persistent perturbation along the updated critical boundary still forces chattering and collision.
  2. Rotational Unwinding & Chattering on $\text{SO}(3)$ ($\mathbb{S}^3$ Double Cover): Every physical 3D orientation corresponds to two antipodal unit quaternions, $q_e$ and $-q_e$, representing opposite rotation directions around the Euler axis. A continuous controller on $\text{SO}(3)$ either suffers from unwinding (rotating almost $360^\circ$ when near $\eta_e \approx -1$) or introduces a discontinuous sign flip across the equator $\eta_e = 0$ (where the error angle is $180^\circ$). Under attitude noise $\varepsilon_q > 0$ near $\eta_e = 0$, a standard RL policy chatters violently between opposing angular velocity commands $(u_y, u_z)$, stalling at an unaligned attitude:
$\text{SO}(3)$ Attitude Tracking Under Adversarial Noise ($\varepsilon_q = 0.4$): Left (Standard PPO, $\delta_\eta = 0$): Initial orientations near the antipodal equator ($\eta_e(0) \in \{0, \pm 0.13\}$) are trapped at $\eta_e = 0$ as angular velocity commands $(u_y, u_z)$ chatter at maximum frequency. Right (L-MultiHyRL with Quaternion Hysteresis, $\delta_\eta = 0.2$): Hysteresis locks in the active pole $\vartheta \in \{-1, 1\}$, eliminating control chattering and smoothly stabilizing all initial orientations.

2. How L-MultiHyRL Works: Latent Partitioning & Dual Hysteresis

Starting from a single baseline RL policy $\pi_0$ trained via PPO, L-MultiHyRL robustifies 3D navigation in three steps without requiring global obstacle coordinates or dynamics rollouts at runtime:

Step 1: Critical Point Identification & SVD Hyperplane Partitioning

To locate the vulnerable states of $\pi_0$ around an obstacle, we roll out pairs of trajectories fromperturbed positions $p \pm \nu$ across a 3D grid. Wherever opposing perturbations cause the trajectories to diverge onto opposite sides of the obstacle ($d_H(\phi_1, \phi_2) > \mu^$), we label the initial state as a critical point $z \in \mathcal{M}^$.

Rather than using complex 2D Voronoi tessellations (de Priester et al., 2024), we compute the centroid $p^c$ of $\mathcal{M}^*$ and apply Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) to fit an optimal bisecting hyperplane with normal vector $\vec{n}_c$ (panel b below). Shifting this plane by $\pm \rho$ creates two overlapping 3D half-spaces $\mathcal{M}_1^{\text{exp}}$ and $\mathcal{M}_2^{\text{exp}}$ of overlap thickness $2\rho > 2\varepsilon_p$, on which we fine-tune two specialized policies $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$ initialized directly from $\pi_0$:

Geometric Partitioning of 3D Critical Points: (a) Opposing perturbations $p \pm \nu$ cause baseline trajectories $\phi_1, \phi_2$ to diverge around the obstacle, identifying critical points. (b) Red dots show the empirical critical set $\mathcal{M}^*$; SVD fits a bisecting plane through the centroid $p^c$ and expands it by $\pm \rho$ to form the overlapping region $\mathcal{M}_1^{\text{exp}} \cap \mathcal{M}_2^{\text{exp}}$ (pink). (c) Full 3D view of the overlapping domain ahead of the obstacle.

Step 2: Latent Space Membership Prediction (Zero Redundant Encoders)

At runtime, the vehicle does not know global obstacle coordinates—it only sees its local visual projections $\mathcal{V}(z)$. Instead of training a separate CNN classifier from scratch as in MultiHyRL (de Priester et al., 2024), L-MultiHyRL reuses the baseline policy’s frozen convolutional feature extractor $\Phi_{\text{CNN}}$. A lightweight multi-layer perceptron $g_\psi$ takes the 64-dimensional latent visual embedding $h_{\mathcal{V}}(z) = \Phi_{\text{CNN}}(\mathcal{V}(z))$ and directly predicts membership $\hat{m}(z) = (\hat{m}_1, \hat{m}_2) \in {0, 1}^2$ in the overlapping regions $\mathcal{M}_1^{\text{exp}}$ and $\mathcal{M}_2^{\text{exp}}$:

Multimodal Network Architecture: The convolutional branch $\Phi_{\text{CNN}}$ compresses the orthogonal visual projections $\mathcal{V} = (\mathcal{V}_{xy}, \mathcal{V}_{xz})$ into a 64-D latent representation $h_{\mathcal{V}}$, which is concatenated ($\oplus$) with vector observations $o_{\text{vec}}$ for control and reused directly by the latent partition classifier $g_\psi$.

Step 3: Discretized Closed-Loop Hybrid System with Dual Hysteresis

At each sampling instant $t_k$, the deployed controller operates as a discretized hybrid system $\mathcal{H}_s = (C, F_s, D, G)$ with state $x := (z, \lambda, \vartheta)$, where two independent discrete memory states prevent chattering across both topological obstructions simultaneously:

  • Spatial Policy Hysteresis ($\lambda \in {1, 2}$): Inside the overlap $\mathcal{M}1^{\text{exp}} \cap \mathcal{M}_2^{\text{exp}}$ (where $\hat{m}(z) = (1, 1)$) or in free space ($\hat{m}(z) = (0, 0)$), the supervisor keeps the active policy $\pi\lambda$ constant ($\lambda^+ = \lambda$). It switches to $\lambda^+ = 3 - \lambda$ only when the vehicle exits the overlap into the exclusive region of the opposing policy ($\hat{m}\lambda(z) = 0$ and $\hat{m}{3-\lambda}(z) = 1$).
  • Quaternion Pole Hysteresis ($\vartheta \in {-1, 1}$): Rather than flipping the target quaternion pole at $\eta_e = 0$, the supervisor tracks pole $\vartheta$ and switches to $\vartheta^+ = -\vartheta$ only when the scalar quaternion alignment crosses the hysteresis threshold $\vartheta \eta_e \le -\delta_\eta$ (with $\delta_\eta \in (0, 1)$).
Dual Hysteresis Switching Logic in $\mathcal{H}_s$: (a) Attitude Hysteresis on $\mathbb{S}^3$: The target pole $\vartheta \in \{-1, 1\}$ only switches when $\eta_e$ crosses outside the hysteresis band $[-\delta_\eta, +\delta_\eta]$, preventing equatorial chattering. (b) Spatial Hysteresis in $\mathbb{R}^3$: The active policy $\lambda \in \{1, 2\}$ only switches when leaving the overlapping region $\mathcal{M}_1^{\text{exp}} \cap \mathcal{M}_2^{\text{exp}}$.

3. 3D Simulation & Physical Quadrotor Validation

We evaluate L-MultiHyRL against a continuous Standard PPO baseline across 1,000 randomized 3D initial conditions in both single-obstacle and multi-obstacle arenas under worst-case adversarial observation perturbations $\varepsilon_p \in [0, 0.20]\text{ m}$, followed by zero-shot sim-to-real deployment on a physical ModalAI Sentinel quadrotor.

3D Multi-Obstacle Simulation Benchmark

As shown below, when adversarial noise ($\varepsilon_p = 0.20\text{ m}$) shifts the perceived obstacle positions across the critical dividing plane, Standard PPO (red dashed trajectories) chatters between opposing bypass directions and repeatedly crashes into the spherical obstacles (black circles). In contrast, L-MultiHyRL (solid blue for $\lambda = 1$, solid green for $\lambda = 2$) locks in a committed bypass mode upon entering each obstacle’s latent overlap zone, achieving a 100% success rate across all perturbation levels:

3D Multi-Obstacle Navigation Under Adversarial Observation Perturbations ($\varepsilon_p = 0.20\text{ m}$): Top-down ($p_x$-$p_y$, left) and side ($p_x$-$p_z$, right) projections. Standard PPO (red dashed) chatters and collides with the obstacles (black circles), while L-MultiHyRL smoothly routes all trajectories to the target (gold star) via policies $\lambda = 1$ (blue) and $\lambda = 2$ (green).
Cumulative Success Rate Over Time (1,000 Runs per Condition): As the adversarial perturbation magnitude increases from $\varepsilon_p = 0.00$ to $\varepsilon_p = 0.20$, Standard PPO (solid curves) degrades to 60.4% success in the single-obstacle arena and 68.6% in the multi-obstacle arena. L-MultiHyRL (dashed curves) maintains 100.0% collision-free convergence across all noise levels.

Physical Flight Tests on a ModalAI Sentinel Quadrotor

To validate real-world performance under physical aerodynamic disturbances and closed-loop tracking delays, we deployed L-MultiHyRL zero-shot onto a ModalAI Sentinel quadrotor inside an OptiTrack motion-capture arena ($50\text{ Hz}$ outer-loop policy updates feeding PX4 velocity and yaw-rate setpoints).

Physical Quadrotor Multi-Obstacle Flights Under Perturbations ($\varepsilon_p = 0.25\text{ m}$): Top-down ($p_x$-$p_y$, left) and side ($p_x$-$p_z$, right) flight paths recorded on the ModalAI Sentinel quadrotor. Standard PPO (red) suffers from mid-flight indecision and collides with the obstacles (black circles), whereas L-MultiHyRL (blue/green) executes decisive, collision-free 3D avoidance maneuvers in every flight trial.
Physical Quadrotor $\text{SO}(3)$ Attitude Tracking Under Perturbations ($\varepsilon_q = 0.4$): Left (Standard PPO, $\delta_\eta = 0$): Initial orientations near the antipodal boundary ($\eta_e(0) \in \{0, \pm 0.13\}$) trigger severe hardware angular rate chattering and stall near $\eta_e = 0$. Right (L-MultiHyRL, $\delta_\eta = 0.2$): Quaternion pole hysteresis eliminates chattering on the physical quadrotor and drives all trajectories smoothly to the desired orientation.

🛠️ Key Engineering Takeaway: Evolution of the HyRL Framework

From 2D state-based HyRL (ACC 2022) (de Priester et al., 2022) and 2D vision-based MultiHyRL (RLC 2024) (de Priester et al., 2024) to 3D L-MultiHyRL (2026) (de Priester et al., 2026), our core insight remains simple: continuous neural networks cannot robustly break symmetry around topological obstructions without discrete memory. By extracting overlapping region classifiers directly from a policy's existing CNN latent space and embedding dual spatial and quaternion hysteresis, L-MultiHyRL upgrades standard deep RL policies into provably chatter-free 3D flight controllers with negligible runtime overhead.

Specifically, when bypassing obstacles in SE(3) or tracking attitudes in SO(3), topological obstructions create decision boundaries where perturbations can induce rapid switching (chattering) between passage routes or rotation directions, trapping the vehicle at unaligned attitudes or causing collisions. To resolve these vulnerabilities, we propose Latent Multi-Policy Hysteresis-Based RL (L-MultiHyRL) for robust, perception-driven obstacle avoidance in SE(3). L-MultiHyRL identifies the critical points of a baseline RL policy, partitions the state space into overlapping contractible sets, predicts partition membership directly from learned local CNN features, and orchestrates switching via a discretized closed-loop hybrid system with dual spatial and quaternion hysteresis. Validated in 3D simulations and physical ModalAI Sentinel quadrotor flights.
